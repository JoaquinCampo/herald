\section{The \texttt{herald-logits} Corpus}
\label{sec:corpus}

Equations~\ref{eq:damage} and~\ref{eq:risk} ask for three things at scale:
for every prompt, a full-cache run and a compressed run scored on the same
task; for every step of every compressed run, the causal statistics that
form \info; and, to test the mechanism hypothesis rather than assume it, a
measurement of how far the compressed model's distributions have actually
drifted from the full-cache model's at each step. Producing these requires
decoding every prompt once uncompressed and once per compression action,
with the uncompressed model re-evaluated along every compressed trajectory.
That is expensive to do once and pointless to do twice, so we did it once and
released the result. \texttt{herald-logits} is a public corpus from which any
compression-damage target, including ours, can be defined without rerunning a
model.

\paragraph{Construction.}
All generations use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct with greedy decoding and a
task-dependent token budget. Prompts come from four tasks that span
arithmetic reasoning, code, instruction following, and long-context reading:
GSM8K, HumanEval, IFEval, and the single-document subset of LongBench
(\todo{per-task prompt counts}; 764 prompts in total). Each prompt is decoded
43 times: once with the full cache, and once under each of six KV-cache
compressors at each of seven removal ratios,
$r \in \{0.25, 0.375, 0.5, 0.75, 0.875, 0.9375, 0.96875\}$. The compressors
are StreamingLLM, SnapKV, ExpectedAttention, KNorm, TOVA, and random
eviction, all through the kvpress implementations, so that differences
between them are differences in eviction policy and not in harness. This
yields 32{,}852 sequences and 7{,}738{,}974 compressed token rows.

\paragraph{Per-token signals.}
Every compressed token row carries twelve statistics of the model's
next-token distribution at that step, each computable from the logits the
decoder has already produced: entropy, top-1 and top-5 probability, the
number of plausible alternatives, average log-probability, entropy change
from the previous step, KL divergence from the previous step's distribution,
top-10 overlap with the previous step, effective vocabulary size, tail
probability mass, and logit range. These are the raw material for \info; we
call them zero-cost because a serving stack computes the logits regardless,
and every statistic is a constant-time reduction over them. The release also
ships rolling means over 8- and 32-token windows and exponential moving
averages at the same half-lives. Position fields record the absolute token
index; the release additionally stores relative progress, which we treat as
forbidden for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:target}.

\paragraph{Oracles.}
For every compressed step the corpus stores the divergence between the
compressed model's next-token distribution and the full-cache model's
distribution evaluated on the same prefix by teacher forcing. Conditioning
both on identical tokens isolates the effect of the compressed cache from
ordinary autoregressive drift: it asks what the intact model would have
predicted right here. Jensen--Shannon and KL variants are stored per step,
together with forward-looking sums and maxima over horizons of 5, 10, 25, and
50 tokens. These oracles require the full-cache model and are therefore never
available at inference time; in this paper they are measurement, never input.

\paragraph{Task scores and the damage label.}
Each sequence carries the task's own score for its completed output:
\todo{exact-match final answer for GSM8K, functional pass for HumanEval,
instruction-level accuracy for IFEval, and the LongBench reference metric;
verify each against the label audit}. The full-cache run's score and the
compressed run's score together give \dmg{} by Equation~\ref{eq:damage}. The
corpus also records, per compressed run, two catastrophe detectors that need
no reference: \emph{looping}, when any window of 20 contiguous tokens
recurs at least three times in the output, with onset at the second
occurrence; and \emph{non-termination}, when decoding ended by exhausting the
token budget rather than by an end-of-sequence token. Looping almost always
escalates into non-termination, since a model committed to a loop has no
mechanism to break out. We use the flags to split damaged runs into
catastrophic and graceful for the earliness analysis; they are not inputs and
not the label.

\paragraph{How much damage there is, and where.}
Table~\ref{tab:prevalence} reports the damage rate under
Equation~\ref{eq:damage} by compressor and by ratio, alongside the
catastrophe rate. Two features of the distribution shape everything
downstream. First, damage is highly compressor-specific on identical prompts
and ratios: the spread between the most fragile and the most robust
compressor is \todo{about an order of magnitude}, and \todo{two} of the
principled compressors are worse than random eviction. Second, damage grows
steadily with the ratio, roughly \todo{ten points per 0.125 step} pooled
across compressors, so that at the heaviest ratios damage is the modal
outcome rather than a tail event. Averaged over a benchmark, these are
percentages; for a serving system, they say that neither the compressor nor
the ratio alone tells you whether \emph{this} generation is at risk, which is
the gap the forecaster fills. The full-cache model's own error sets the floor
the label is defined against: on GSM8K it produces a wrong final answer on
\todo{22.4\%} of prompts with no compression, and those prompts can only be
damaged if the compressed run does strictly worse.

\paragraph{Release.}
The corpus is released under the MIT license in three configurations:
\texttt{tokens} (one row per compressed token), \texttt{sequences} (one row
per run with prompt, output, prediction, ground truth, stop reason, scores,
and catastrophe flags), and \texttt{summaries} (one row per compressor,
ratio, and task with aggregate metrics). Benchmark prompts are redistributed
under their original licenses.

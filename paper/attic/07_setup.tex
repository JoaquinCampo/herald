\section{Experimental Setup and Decision Rules}

Splits are prompt disjoint: every ratio and switch position from a prompt
belongs to one split, and early stopping and model selection use training
prompts only. The final row counts are \todo{training rows},
\todo{validation rows}, and \todo{held-out rows} per compressor. This split
prevents correlated positions from a single prompt from appearing on both
sides of the held-out evaluation.

Each $(\text{prompt}, \text{ratio})$ trajectory receives equal total training
mass. If trajectory $i$ has $n_i$ switch rows, its row $j$ has weight
\begin{equation}
w_{ij}=\frac{1}{n_i}.
\end{equation}
Evaluation macro-averages ratios, so unequal trajectory lengths or coverage
cannot change the influence of a ratio.

Primary evidence is held-out-prompt MSE and RMSE skill over the strongest
grouped-mean baseline, MAE skill over the strongest grouped-median baseline,
and prompt-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals. Diagnostics report
calibration by predicted-damage bins; positive, zero, and negative label
prevalence; error on positive and non-zero rows; mean prediction versus mean
target; and rank correlation. Results are reported separately by compressor
and ratio, because a pooled result cannot rescue a failed compressor.

A compressor supports the magnitude claim only when all of the following
preregistered conditions hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item its 95\% prompt-bootstrap interval for MSE skill is strictly above
  zero;
  \item error on positive-damage rows does not worsen;
  \item calibration is graded rather than collapsed near zero; and
  \item per-ratio results show that one setting does not create the aggregate
  gain.
\end{enumerate}
Otherwise, the current features do not establish magnitude forecasting for
that compressor. Any-damage and major-damage risk are preregistered secondary
endpoints. Their thresholds and metrics must be frozen before primary results
are inspected; \todo{state the frozen secondary definitions and thresholds.}

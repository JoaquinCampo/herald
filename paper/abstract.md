# Abstract

Heavy KV-cache compression makes large language models fail catastrophically: they get stuck in loops or fail to terminate. We show these failures are highly predictable from the model's own logits, dozens of tokens before they become visible. We introduce HERALD, a lightweight XGBoost classifier trained on zero-cost per-token logit features. Across six KV-cache compression methods applied to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on GSM8K, it forecasts catastrophic failures with AUROC 0.97, generalizes to held-out compressors via leave-one-compressor-out evaluation, and retains AUROC 0.88 in the pre-onset regime, when no failure is yet visible, compared to a rolling-entropy baseline that barely beats chance. These results show that catastrophic compression failures emit a clear warning signal well before they become observable, opening a path to runtime intervention with negligible compute overhead.

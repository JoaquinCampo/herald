# Understanding after the failed B16 replication

Keep signed final quality loss as the target. A binary or discontinuous realized
outcome does not make its conditional expectation ill-defined, so the failed
linear predictor is not grounds for replacing the target with an easier proxy.
Do not infer impossibility from one fixed task, action or measurement protocol.

A bounded primary-source refresh found two useful distinctions.
[KVDiagnosis](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09412v1) reports paired all-source
compression outcomes and diagnostic separation of correct-to-wrong failures
from correct-to-correct controls. Its strongest measurements include gold-rank
shift and paired likelihood traces. Those results are diagnostic, not a frozen
pre-action signed-loss predictor. Repeated sources also limit the interpretation
of its reported intervals. Post-compression measurement is not automatically
inadmissible: a paid cloned-state probe can precede a live action. Future tokens
and gold annotations still require an explicit admissibility and cost account.
The public [repository](https://github.com/ChosenQC/KVDiagnosis) can inform a
measurement audit; no outcome ledger or dataset has been downloaded or reused.

[The Pitfalls of KV Cache Compression](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00231v1)
reports instruction-order effects and disproportionate eviction across separate
instructions. Rebalancing retention and protecting selected keywords improve
its studied behaviors. This supplies a concrete task-level mechanism that our
single-needle assay does not vary. It does not establish a risk predictor, and
Knorm's comparatively balanced eviction does not itself preserve semantics.

Next bounded understanding step: inspect the official instruction-following
workload and scoring contract, then decide whether instruction competition and
component-specific eviction can yield an admissible, cheaply observed predictor.
No new GPU collection, model, compression budget or numerical gate is selected.
Preserve the current goal and require a prospective protocol before execution.

Competing hypotheses for that inspection:

1. Instruction type and order explain most loss, so a prompt-only baseline suffices.
2. Action-specific eviction imbalance adds information beyond those baselines.
3. Losing a few decisive tokens matters more than average span retention, making
   the proposed mean-retention mechanism inadequate even with known spans.
4. Full-cache competence or metric artifacts dominate, making this workload
   unsuitable for a paired signed-loss study without changing the assay.
5. Existing evidence is mainly compressor-level aggregation and will not transfer
   to unseen prompt groups. A prospective failure would be informative, not a
   reason to retune exposed rows until a metric improves.

The next decision must distinguish these explanations before building collectors.
Use Luna for this bounded source and data-contract inspection; no Sol escalation
is warranted merely because the previous hypothesis failed.
